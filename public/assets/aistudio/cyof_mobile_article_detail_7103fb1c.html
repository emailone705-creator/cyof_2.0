The Architecture of Solitude and Grace

A monograph on the unhurried craft of double-faced cashmere and sculptural silhouetting inside our Biella workshops.

Words by Hélène de Saint-Germain Biella, IT
Photography by Marco Bellini 14 Oct 2025 • 7 Min

To enter the Piedmontese valley of Biella in the quiet haze of late autumn is to understand why time in luxury is not a constraint, but an organic ingredient. Here, surrounded by water so mineral-pure it relaxes the coiled elasticity of virgin Andean fibres, the artisan does not cut to conquer the cloth. Instead, there is a slow conversation between sheared fleece and hands that have worked the looms for three ancestral generations.

In our contemporary atelier, the double-faced mantle exists as an architectural triumph over artificial reinforcement. Unlike conventional tailoring, which relies on synthetic fusing, thermo-bonding interfacings, and rigid horsehair canvas to simulate form, the double-splittable wool requires an entirely monastic dedication: two wafer-thin layers of 14.2-micron cashmere woven together by invisible tying threads, then split open by blade precisely half an inch at every hem.

“A true garment does not assert itself in motion; it commands space when standing perfectly still.”
— Maestro Giancarlo Valli, Head Tailor

The edges are manually folded inward upon themselves and blind-stitched with single-filament silk thread. A single coat consumes thirty-six dedicated hours of continuous hand-tucking. To inspect the seam under magnification is to see no visible trace of puncture; the stitches sleep inside the cellular marrow of the cloth.
